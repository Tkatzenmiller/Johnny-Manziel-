1992–2006

Early life

Johnathan Paul Manziel was born on December 6, 1992, in Tyler, Texas, to Paul and Michelle Manziel. He has a younger sister, Meri. The family settled in Kerrville in the Texas Hill Country, where he played football, baseball, basketball and golf as a kid.

2006–2010

Kerrville & Tivy High

At Tivy High School, coached by Mark Smith, he started as a receiver before becoming a dual-threat quarterback. Over three varsity seasons he threw for 7,500 yards and 75 touchdowns and ran for 4,038 yards and 78 more. As a senior in 2010 he won Mr. Texas Football and was named a Parade All-American and the National High School Coaches Association's football athlete of the year.

He first committed to Oregon, then switched to Texas A&M. He also held offers from Baylor, Stanford, Rice, Iowa State and others. He graduated from Tivy in 2010.

2011–2013

Texas A&M: Johnny Football

He enrolled at A&M in January 2011 and redshirted that fall. In June 2012 he was arrested after a fight in College Station's Northgate bar district, and the school briefly suspended him before a dean overturned it. Weeks later coach Kevin Sumlin named him the starter for A&M's first season in the SEC.

What followed was one of college football's great seasons. He led a 29–24 upset of No. 1 Alabama in Tuscaloosa, won the Heisman as the first freshman ever to do so, and finished with a Cotton Bowl-record 516 total yards in a 41–13 rout of Oklahoma. His 5,116 total yards set SEC and NCAA freshman records.

Fame came with scrutiny. Before the 2013 season the NCAA investigated reports that he had been paid to sign autographs, which led to a first-half suspension against Rice. He still threw for 4,114 yards and 37 touchdowns in 2013, won the Chick-fil-A Bowl with a comeback against Duke, and declared for the NFL draft in January 2014.

2014–2016

Cleveland Browns

The Browns traded up to take him 22nd overall in 2014. He backed up Brian Hoyer as a rookie and struggled in two late-season starts. In February 2015 he entered a treatment program and spent about ten weeks at a rehab center in Pennsylvania, then publicly retired his money-sign celebration.

In 2015 he showed flashes, including a win over Tennessee and a 372-yard game against Pittsburgh, but a bye-week party video got him demoted. He returned as starter in December, suffered a concussion, and was reported to be in Las Vegas the weekend of the season finale. Cleveland waived him on March 11, 2016, after 15 games, 8 starts and a 2–6 record.

2016–2017

The fall

2016 was his lowest year. A January incident with ex-girlfriend Colleen Crowley led to an indictment for misdemeanor assault; he denied wrongdoing and the case was dismissed in November 2017 after he met the terms of an agreement. His agents and Nike dropped him, and the NFL suspended him four games for a substance-abuse violation even though he had no team.

In Netflix's 2023 documentary he said that after the 2015 season he was using OxyContin and cocaine daily, and that he attempted suicide after his release. In 2017 he was diagnosed with bipolar disorder and began treatment.

2018–2022

Comeback attempts

In February 2018 he told Good Morning America about his diagnosis, said he had been self-medicating with alcohol, and said he had stopped drinking. He married Bre Tiesi in March 2018. That year he played in the CFL for Hamilton and Montreal, but the league released and barred him in February 2019 for breaking the terms of his contract. He played briefly for the AAF's Memphis Express before that league folded, then joined the fan-run Fan Controlled Football league in 2021 and 2022. He and Tiesi split in 2019; their divorce was finalized in 2021.

2022–today

Life now

Texas A&M inducted him into its Athletics Hall of Fame in 2022. In August 2023 Untold: Johnny Football premiered on Netflix and became the service's top film in the U.S. within a day. He said the documentary closed his football chapter.

Since then he has talked openly about his past in long interviews, including Shannon Sharpe's Club Shay Shay in 2024. He launched his own interview podcast, Glory Daze, in fall 2024. He lives in Scottsdale, Arizona, where he spends much of his time golfing. In 2025 he appeared on Fox's Special Forces: World's Toughest Test, and in May 2026 he won an amateur MMA bout by first-round TKO, then said he probably would not fight again.
